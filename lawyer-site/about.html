The advocate

A practice built on returning phone calls

Rezaul Karim read law at the University of Dhaka, taking an LL.B. (Hons) in 2005 and an LL.M. in civil and commercial law in 2006. He was enrolled as an advocate with the Bangladesh Bar Council in 2008, admitted to the High Court Division in 2012, and to the Appellate Division in 2018.

The first six years were spent in the district courts of Dhaka and Gazipur, doing the unglamorous work — title searches, execution proceedings, bail applications at eight in the morning. That grounding is why this chamber is unusually careful about documents: most cases in Bangladesh are won or lost on the record long before anyone stands up to argue.

Today the practice divides roughly in three: family and inheritance matters, land and title disputes, and company and employment work for small and mid-sized businesses. A steady stream of writ petitions runs alongside, usually against a licensing or service decision that nobody bothered to explain to the person it affected.

He appears in Bangla and English, and writes to clients in whichever they read more comfortably.

Representative work

Matters of the kind this chamber handles

Illustrative, anonymised and shown with permission. Every case turns on its own facts; past outcomes are not a promise of future ones.

Eleven-year mutation error corrected

A family in Savar held a valid deed but the khatian named a stranger. Rather than a fresh title suit, the chamber traced the error to a 2013 mutation order and moved the correct forum for correction. Record rectified in fourteen months.

Dower and maintenance recovered in full

Family court suit for unpaid dower and maintenance, with a parallel execution plan prepared before judgment so recovery did not stall. Full amount realised within five months of decree.

Licence cancellation set aside by writ

A trading licence was cancelled without notice or hearing. Writ petition under Article 102 argued on natural justice grounds; the order was declared without lawful authority and the business reopened.

How this chamber works

Four rules that do not bend

Everything in writing

Advice, fees and next steps are confirmed by email or letter, so there is never a dispute about what was promised.

A reply within 48 hours

Every message gets an answer inside two working days, even if the answer is that the matter is still under consideration.

No case taken to fill a diary

If your matter is weak, outside this chamber’s areas, or better settled, you will be told at the outset and referred onward.

Your file stays private

Documents are kept under lock and, in digital form, on encrypted storage. Nothing is discussed outside the chamber without your consent.

Tell the chamber what happened

Ten minutes on the form, a written answer in two working days, and no fee until you decide to proceed.
